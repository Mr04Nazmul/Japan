THE MYJAPAN FIELD GUIDE

Make a Japan travel map that tells your story

A travel map is most useful when it reflects the places you actually remember. myjapan is a personal prefecture tracker and poster maker created by Hossain Najmul. Use the interactive map above to record your trips, find gaps in your journey and save a picture of your progress. You can use the map without an account.

1. Decide what a visit means to you

Choose a consistent rule before marking your map. You might count a prefecture after spending time in a town, visiting a local attraction or staying overnight. A train connection or drive through the area may not feel like a visit to you. The app does not enforce a definition: use the same personal rule each time so your progress remains meaningful.

2. Build your map, one place at a time

Tap a prefecture on the map to mark or unmark it. If a small shape is difficult to select, switch to List or use the prefecture search below the map. Region filters narrow the list; they do not erase your existing selections. The visited count and percentage update as you make changes. The percentage counts prefectures equally: it is not a measure of distance travelled, land area covered or time spent in Japan.

3. Turn the gaps into your next trip

Start with one unvisited prefecture and choose a single base for your trip. Write down one place you want to see, one experience you want to try and a practical return journey. Check current transport times and opening hours with the operators before booking. A compact trip with time to explore can make a better memory than a rushed itinerary designed only to colour more of the map.

If you live in Japan, you can build your map gradually through day trips and weekends. If you are visiting for a short holiday, keep a separate list of places you would like to return to. The map records whether you have visited a prefecture; it cannot show how deeply you explored it. Your notes, photos and memories give that coloured shape its meaning.

4. Create a keepsake you can read

Enter your name and a short journey title, then choose Download picture. Review the preview before saving. PNG is useful for sharing as an ordinary image; the SVG option keeps the map sharp when enlarged. On an iPhone, try Save to Gallery and the system share menu. If the browser does not offer image saving, open the PNG and use the browser's image-save option. Keep names short enough to fit comfortably on your poster.

5. Keep your progress safe

Your name and visited selections are stored in this browser on this device. They do not automatically sync to another phone or browser. Clearing browser data, using private browsing or changing devices can remove or separate your progress. Save a poster after an important update as a visual record. Reset map clears the visited selection after confirmation; you can also tap individual places to correct a mistake.

Questions about the map

Can I correct an accidental selection?
Yes. Tap the same prefecture again to unmark it. Use the list if the map shape is difficult to select.
Does my percentage show how much of Japan I have seen?
It shows your selected prefectures divided by 47, rounded for display. It does not score your travel experience.
Is this a route planner or live travel service?
No. It is a personal record and poster tool. Confirm routes, prices, closures and travel conditions with official providers.
Where can I report a problem?
Visit About & contact for the project contact route. Please describe your browser and the action that failed without sharing private travel information.
